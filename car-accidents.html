Motor-vehicle collision care

Evaluation after a car accident

Pain and stiffness may develop after a motor-vehicle collision. A thoughtful medical evaluation can help clarify symptoms and next steps.

What to expect

Patients may report neck, back, shoulder or other musculoskeletal symptoms after a crash. The clinician can review the mechanism of injury, medical history, current concerns and functional impact.

Preparing for your visit

  • Bring a current medication list and relevant prior medical records, if available.
  • Be ready to describe symptoms, activity limitations and when the injury occurred.
  • Ask our scheduling team what records or authorizations may be required.

Questions before scheduling?

Seek emergency care immediately after a collision for severe pain, weakness, confusion, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness or other concerning symptoms. For insurance and personal-injury questions, call the office to discuss documentation requirements; no legal outcome is implied.

Evaluation after a car accident — Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common patient questions. Recommendations depend on your individual medical evaluation.

What symptoms should be checked after a car crash?

Neck or back pain, headaches, limited movement, numbness or weakness may develop after a collision. Severe headache, confusion, fainting, chest pain, trouble breathing or new neurological deficits require emergency care.

Can symptoms start a day or two after an accident?

Yes. Some musculoskeletal symptoms can become more noticeable hours or days after a collision. A medical evaluation can document symptoms and examine for injuries; do not delay urgent care when warning signs are present.

What should I bring to a post-collision appointment?

Bring accident and symptom dates, relevant emergency-department notes, imaging reports, medication list and any insurance or referral information. You do not need to bring a police report unless the office specifically requests it.

Does ReMD provide emergency accident care?

No. A rehabilitation clinic is not a substitute for emergency evaluation. If there are serious or rapidly worsening symptoms, call 911 or visit an emergency department.
