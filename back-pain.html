Understanding back pain evaluation

A clinical assessment looks at the onset and pattern of pain, symptoms into the legs, previous injuries, medical history and any changes in movement or strength. Your physician discusses whether additional assessment or treatment is appropriate.

What to expect

During a visit, the physician may discuss the history of your symptoms, past treatment, relevant records, and any activity limitations. The examination and next steps depend on your medical needs.

To help prepare, bring a medication list, relevant imaging reports or test results if available, and any referral or authorization paperwork requested by the scheduling team.

Frequently asked questions

Back Pain Evaluation FAQs

When should I seek medical care for back pain?

Back pain can affect mobility, sleep, work and everyday activity. A clinical assessment looks at the onset and pattern of pain, symptoms into the legs, previous injuries, medical history and any changes in movement or strength. Your physician discusses whether additional assessment or treatment is appropriate.

Can a work injury cause back pain?

Yes. Lifting or carrying heavy objects, a slip or fall, repetitive bending, prolonged awkward positions, or a sudden workplace accident can strain muscles and other structures in the back. Symptoms may begin immediately or develop over time. Tell the physician how the injury happened, which job duties make symptoms worse, and whether pain, numbness, or weakness affects your work. An evaluation can help determine the next appropriate steps and whether work-activity modifications should be considered.

What can a physician assess during an appointment?

During a back pain visit, the physician may review your symptoms and injury history, assess posture and spinal movement, and check strength, sensation, reflexes, and walking when appropriate. The examination may help identify signs of muscle or joint problems and possible nerve involvement. Based on the findings, the physician can discuss whether imaging, electrodiagnostic testing, rehabilitation, or other care is indicated. Not every patient needs every test.
